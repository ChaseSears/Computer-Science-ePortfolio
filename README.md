# Welcome to my Computer Science ePortfolio

This ePortfolio represents my work throughout the Bachelor of Science in Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University. It demonstrates my growth in software design and engineering, algorithms and data structures, and database development.

## Professional Self-Assessment
Throughout my time in the Bachelor of Science in Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have developed the technical knowledge and problem-solving skills necessary to pursue a career in information technology and software development. My education has provided me with experience in programming, software engineering, algorithms, data structures, database management, and cybersecurity. It has also helped me understand how these different areas of computer science work together to create reliable and efficient systems. Completing my capstone ePortfolio has allowed me to reflect on my progress throughout the program and demonstrate the skills I have developed through practical projects. My experiences in the United States Army and the manufacturing industry have also shaped my approach to problem-solving, teamwork, and professional development.

Before pursuing my degree, I served four years in the United States Army as a combat engineer (12B). During my military service, I learned the importance of discipline, teamwork, communication, and adapting to difficult situations. Working in an environment where safety and attention to detail were essential taught me how to approach problems systematically and make decisions under pressure. After leaving the military, I began working in the manufacturing industry and eventually became a crane operator at AM/NS Calvert. Throughout my time in the steel mill, I have gained experience with industrial operations, production processes, and the computer systems used to manage materials and coordinate production. These experiences have given me a unique perspective on the relationship between technology and the people who depend on it. They have also motivated me to pursue a career where I can combine my knowledge of industrial operations with my education in computer science.

One of the most valuable skills I have developed throughout the Computer Science program is the ability to communicate technical information and understand the needs of different stakeholders. In CS 255: System Analysis and Design, I worked on the DriverPass project, which involved identifying business requirements and developing system designs for a driver training application. This project required me to consider the needs of students, instructors, and administrators while creating diagrams and documentation that explained how the proposed system would operate. It helped me understand the importance of gathering requirements, organizing information, and communicating technical solutions in a way that different audiences can understand. My experience in the Army and manufacturing industry has reinforced these skills because both environments require effective communication and cooperation to accomplish shared goals. Although many of my programming assignments were completed independently, my professional experience has prepared me to contribute to collaborative environments where technical decisions must account for the needs of multiple people.

Software engineering has been one of the areas where I have experienced the most growth. Throughout my coursework, I have worked with several programming languages, including Java, Python, C++, and JavaScript. I have also gained experience using development environments and tools such as Android Studio, Visual Studio, Visual Studio Code, and Git. These experiences have taught me that developing software involves much more than writing code that works. Software must also be organized, maintainable, secure, and designed with the user in mind. In CS 465, I worked on the Travlr application, which used Angular, Express, Node.js, and MongoDB to create a full-stack travel management system. This project helped me understand how frontend and backend components communicate and how authentication can be incorporated into an application. In CS 320: Software Testing, Automation, and Quality Assurance, I also gained experience developing unit tests using JUnit. This reinforced the importance of testing software to identify problems and verify that applications behave as expected.

My understanding of algorithms and data structures has also improved significantly throughout the program. In CS 300: Data Structures and Algorithms, I learned how different data structures can be used to organize and retrieve information efficiently. Working with hash tables and binary search trees demonstrated how the choice of a data structure affects the performance and complexity of a program. I also explored more advanced problem-solving techniques in CS 370: Current and Emerging Trends in Computer Science, where I worked with artificial intelligence and reinforcement learning. These projects helped me understand how algorithms can be designed to solve problems, evaluate possible actions, and improve results. One of the most important lessons I learned is that there is rarely only one way to solve a programming problem. A developer must consider factors such as efficiency, complexity, reliability, and maintainability when deciding which approach is most appropriate.

Database development has been another important part of my education. In CS 340: Client/Server Development, I gained experience working with Python and MongoDB to create applications capable of storing, retrieving, updating, and deleting information. This coursework helped me understand how databases support software applications and why data organization, validation, and efficient queries are important. I also worked with database-driven dashboards that allowed users to interact with stored information. My experience in manufacturing has made this area particularly interesting because accurate information is essential to production operations. At AM/NS Calvert, I use computer systems to track and coordinate steel slabs throughout the production process. Understanding how information moves through these systems has helped me recognize the value of reliable databases and the importance of designing applications that provide accurate information to the people using them.

Cybersecurity has also become an important consideration in how I approach software development. In CS 305: Software Security, I learned about identifying vulnerabilities, evaluating software dependencies, and reducing security risks during development. This course helped me understand that even a functional application can create serious problems if security is not considered during the design process. I expanded on these concepts in CS 465, where I worked with authentication and JSON Web Tokens to protect application functionality. These experiences taught me the importance of validating user input, restricting unauthorized access, and protecting sensitive information. As I continue developing software, I intend to incorporate security considerations throughout the development process rather than treating them as something to address after an application is completed.

Completing my CS 499 capstone has allowed me to apply the knowledge gained throughout the program to projects I previously developed. Instead of simply submitting my original assignments, I revisited them to identify weaknesses and determine how they could be improved. This process required me to review existing code, evaluate design decisions, and apply concepts from multiple courses. It also demonstrated how much my understanding of software development has changed since I originally completed these projects. Features or design decisions that seemed sufficient when I first developed an application could be reconsidered using the knowledge and experience I gained later in the program. The three enhancements included in my ePortfolio represent my growth in software engineering, algorithms and data structures, and database development.

The first artifact in my ePortfolio is my enhanced Event Tracker Android application, originally developed in CS 360: Mobile Architecture and Programming. This enhancement focuses on software design and engineering. The original application allowed users to create accounts and manage events through an Android interface. For the enhancement, I worked on improving the application's functionality, organization, and overall usability. These improvements included strengthening input validation, improving event management features, and refining the application's design. Working on this project demonstrated my ability to revisit an existing application and apply improved software engineering practices. It also reinforced the importance of developing applications that are reliable, understandable, and easier to maintain.

The second artifact is my enhanced ABCU Course Planner, originally developed in CS 300. This enhancement focuses on algorithms and data structures. The original application used a binary search tree to organize course information and allow users to search for courses and view prerequisites. During the enhancement, I expanded the program by incorporating graph-based prerequisite management and implementing Kahn's algorithm to determine a valid order for completing courses. I also incorporated validation to identify invalid prerequisite relationships and detect cycles that would prevent a valid course sequence. This enhancement allowed me to demonstrate a deeper understanding of how algorithms and data structures can be combined to solve more complex problems. It also reinforced the importance of considering efficiency, accuracy, and error handling when designing a solution.

The third artifact is my enhanced Animal Shelter CRUD application, originally developed in CS 340. This enhancement focuses on databases and data management. The original application used Python and MongoDB to perform create, read, update, and delete operations on animal shelter records. During the enhancement process, I focused on improving database functionality, strengthening data validation and error handling, and supporting more efficient retrieval and analysis of stored information. This project helped me better understand the importance of reliable database operations and how the design of a database interface can affect the performance and usability of an application. It also demonstrated my ability to work with database technologies and develop solutions that support applications requiring accurate and accessible information.

Together, these three artifacts demonstrate the technical skills I have developed throughout my Computer Science education. The Event Tracker application highlights my understanding of software design and engineering, the ABCU Course Planner demonstrates my ability to apply algorithms and data structures, and the Animal Shelter application showcases my knowledge of database development. Although each artifact focuses on a different area of computer science, they also demonstrate the importance of problem-solving, code organization, testing, and security across different types of applications. The process of reviewing and enhancing these projects has strengthened my confidence in my ability to evaluate existing software and make improvements based on technical requirements and established development practices.

As I complete my degree, my primary professional goal is to transition from my current position in manufacturing into a career in information technology or software development. I am particularly interested in opportunities involving software systems, database management, and technology used in industrial environments. Having worked directly with production operations, I understand how important reliable computer systems are to maintaining efficiency and preventing disruptions. I believe my experience in manufacturing provides me with an advantage when approaching technical problems because I understand the operational side of the systems that employees rely on every day. My goal is to use this experience alongside my computer science education to help develop, maintain, and improve technology that supports business and production operations.

Overall, my experience at Southern New Hampshire University has helped me grow both technically and professionally. I have developed a stronger understanding of programming, software engineering, algorithms, databases, and cybersecurity while also improving my ability to analyze problems and communicate possible solutions. My military and manufacturing experience has taught me the importance of responsibility, adaptability, and working toward a common goal, while my education has provided me with the technical foundation to apply those qualities in the computer science field. This ePortfolio represents the progress I have made throughout the program and demonstrates my ability to apply what I have learned to practical projects. Although completing my degree is an important accomplishment, I view it as the beginning of my professional development in technology. I intend to continue learning, improving my programming abilities, and pursuing opportunities where I can contribute meaningful solutions to real-world problems.
